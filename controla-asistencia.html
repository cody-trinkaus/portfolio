Section 01Problem Space

In Ecuador, a strict government employee attendance policy creates a rigid and inefficient workplace culture around attendance that does not afford remote work. Therefore, it fails to keep up with the private sector and creates unnecessary barriers and stress for employees to complete their work.

Section 02Understanding the Problem

Because my team was made up of long-time SAE employees, they understood the system well. To get up to speed, I set up formal interviews with SAE employees and had informal conversations about the pain points of the clock-in process.

Section 03Pain Points

Section 04Design Question

How might we improve the clock-in/out experience and enable remote work for SAE employees while adhereing to the strict attendance policies of the Ecuadorian government?

Section 05Ideation and Prototyping

Using Figma, I generated varying prototype fidelities, which I delivered regularly to the lead developer and project manager who gave feedback and guidance on the direction of the next iteration.

Section 06Front-end Development

While designing and prototyping Controla Asistencia, I simultaneously self-taught VUE.js, a Javascript, HTML, and CSS framework. I followed the W3 Schools VUE Tutorial, studied VUE documentation, and watched lots of supplemental videos. To solidify the skills I developed, I hardcoded a prototype of the website.

Hardcoded VUE Prototype. Please note that site quality may have degraded. Plugins, such as the clock that occupied the space on the left of the Teletrabajo page and the data table that occupied the space on the right are no longer functioning.

Section 07Iteration

Designing from scratch proved to be frustrating and time-consuming. However, this exercise revealed the challenges of converting design to deliverable and equipped me to design for development and develop from conceptual designs. As a team, we implemented ReadyKit to streamline front-end development and back-end integration.

I coded the final front-end of the Controla Asistencia using ReadyKit, a collection of template components that utilize VUE and allow for easy integration with a Laravel Back-end.

Section 08Back-end Development

Before working on this application, I had no experience with back-end development. learning on the fly, I simultaneously gained an understanding of Javascript, MYSQL, and Laravel. I am grateful to have a patient feedback-oriented lead developer to lead me through this process in a series of clinics, which I supplemented with Laravel tutorials and lots of documentation reading. The culmination of my learning was programming all of the backend for the pages that I designed.

Back-end development presented me with a unique challenge that prompted massive growth in my programming skills.

Section 09Reflection

This application is more than a project to me. It represents the immense growth that I underwent living and working in Ecuador. Each prototype, page, and mistake in this application correlates to an adventure and skill in my tool belt. Beyond the coding of this application, I filled the gaps between my theoretical knowledge of design and interfaces and the demanding, fast-paced development of an application.

I am a confident designer and developer who would do a million things differently if given the chance to go back in time.

Section 10What I would do differently…

  1. Utilize more formal methods of research and talk to other government agencies about their struggles and innovation.
  2. Create personas and a user journey map.
  3. Define design requirements.
  4. Lay out an in-depth project timeline before beginning.
  5. Spend less time worrying about perfection and more time iterating.
