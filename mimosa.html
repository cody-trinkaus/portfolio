Slow Engagement

A reciprocal relationship between a person and a Mimosa pudica, kept on the plant's schedule, not the person's.

Section 01The Idea

The Mimosa pudica, the sensitivity plant, curls its leaves in response to the time of day, temperature and touch. Our design builds a reciprocal relationship between a person and that plant. It opens its leaves in the morning and closes them at night, and the experience unfolds on the plant's own schedule. That creates a gentle awareness that the plant exists, and invites people to pause and engage with a different rhythm of life.

Section 02How It Works

Three parts carry the experience: ultrasonic sensors, a speaker and an LCD screen. The plant follows its own circadian rhythm, opening and folding its leaves. Ultrasonic sensors measure the distance to a leaf, which lets the design capture that movement. Each movement triggers a chime on the speaker and a message on the screen.

Ultrasonic sensors

We chose them for precise short-range distance sensing, fast read time, and a design that is unobtrusive and non-invasive, so we could watch the plant's natural movement without interfering with it. Those qualities line up with the project's core values: reciprocity, presence and slowness.

A speaker

The chime plays on the plant's schedule, not the human's, which builds slowness into the design. We chose sound because it's the most effective way to invite someone who is busy with their own work to turn toward the plant.

An LCD screen

The screen cycles through affirmations that encourage introspection, emotional awareness and slowness:

"You are growing, even when it's not visible."
"Stillness is not stagnation — it's rest."
"Water yourself — you deserve care, too."
"You don't have to bloom all the time to be worthy."

Section 03The Pot

The plant lives in a pot we designed and 3D printed to house the electronics. It's split in half: the front half holds the plant and the back half holds the computer parts, the Arduino board and the sensors. A protrusion on the front rim mounts the LCD screen where it can be seen, and a drainage hole under the soil section keeps the plant healthy.

Section 04The Garden Marker

To introduce the design, a garden marker sticks out of the soil. It carries the plant's name, how the design works, suggested interactions, and care instructions: indirect sunlight, water until it drains every two to three days, well-draining loamy soil, and fertilizer every two to four weeks in the growing season.

"When it moves, you may hear a wind chime and see a message on the screen. Wake with it in the morning, wind down with it at night. These quiet moments are invitations to pause, reflect, and just be."Garden marker

Section 05Slow Design Lenses

We read the design through Odom's slow design qualities and two slow design principles.

Explicit slowness

Aligning people with the plant's circadian rhythm and senses makes the slowness explicit. When the plant opens or closes in response to time, temperature or touch, a wind chime sounds and the screen offers an affirmation.

Temporal drift

By running on the plant's schedule, the design drops the usual measures of time. The marker invites people to wake up with the plant and go to sleep with it.

Pre-interaction

Nobody knows when a sound will play or which affirmation will appear, so the experience is anticipatory before anything happens.

Thoughtfulness

Temporal drift and explicit slowness cultivate a plant-and-person ecosystem. Caring for the plant, and the affirmations it shows, both encourage reflection and awareness.

Longevity

Ongoing care builds commitment and responsibility, and over time a deeper appreciation of natural rhythms. Unpredictable interactions remind the user that growth is an unfolding process with meaning, presence and care.

Section 06Scenarios

  1. A nice, affirming surprise every day. The screen's messages aim to bring comfort and peace, and to build a routine with the plant that adds meaning to the day.
  2. Environmental plant responses. The plant also responds to touch and temperature. A person can prompt an affirmation by touching its open leaves.
  3. Reflection with the plant's circadian rhythm. When the plant wakes or goes to sleep, the person is invited to spend a mindful moment with it and receive an affirmation.
  4. Plant care. The relationship is reciprocal: care, through watering and feeding, is exchanged for affirmation. Looking after the plant is how a meaningful relationship forms.

Section 07Against Efficiency

The artifact invites people into a shared experience with the Mimosa pudica. The plant expresses itself by folding and opening its leaves, which makes the interaction lived and embodied. It isn't always immediate. When the plant is resting or stressed, the wait for a response asks the user to slow down and observe, which pushes back on the need for instant feedback.

When the plant is stimulated, an affirmation gives a moment to pause and reflect. When it moves, the wind chime follows, drawing attention to the plant's presence and making space for acknowledgement and care.
